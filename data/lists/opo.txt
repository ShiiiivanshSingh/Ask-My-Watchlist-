When recommending or discussing films with me, keep these patterns in mind:

* I’m strongly drawn to stories about **closeness, observation, secrecy, and mediated intimacy**. I often prefer relationships where people watch, follow, overhear, narrate, or communicate indirectly before confronting each other directly.
* I’m fascinated by the gap between **the self someone presents and the self underneath**. Double lives, hidden identities, private obsessions, isolation behind a composed exterior, and characters performing a version of themselves tend to resonate with me.
* I particularly like films that give me **control over interpretation**. I enjoy stories where the meaning, framing, narration, or perspective matters as much as the actual events.
* I like films with **unreliable, retrospective, or unusually intrusive narration**, especially when the narrator changes how I understand events rather than simply explaining them.
* I’m drawn to films where **form and structure are part of the meaning**. Nonlinear storytelling, narration, unusual perspective, compressed time, theatrical staging, or distinctive visual grammar are especially appealing to me.
* I often like **dark or serious themes softened through comedy, absurdism, stylization, or emotional distance**. I can enjoy very heavy material more when the film gives me some formal or comedic cushion.
* I have a strong preference for films that feel **atmospheric, visually deliberate, melancholic, intimate, strange, or slightly unreal** rather than visually ordinary.
* I especially appreciate films where **architecture, locations, objects, framing, color, or production design communicate character and story**, rather than merely providing a backdrop.
* I tend to like stories about **outsiders, lonely people, emotionally displaced people, or characters who feel slightly out of sync with everyone around them**.
* I’m attracted to relationships where intimacy feels **brief, incomplete, fragile, or impossible to fully possess**.
* I often prefer endings that **stop at a threshold** instead of showing the entire aftermath. I like being left with the emotional consequence and space to imagine what happens next.
* I enjoy films where **the ending is not necessarily the main point**. I often care more about how a story reframes what happened, how the characters understand it, and what the structure makes me feel.
* I seem to have a recurring preference for **found or chosen families**, improvised communities, and relationships that temporarily create belonging.
* I like films that can be both **deeply humanistic and slightly playful or absurd**.
* I don't need a film to be conventionally romantic to find the relationships compelling. I’m often more interested in **longing, distance, observation, missed connections, or the possibility of intimacy**.
* I tend to prefer **character-driven cinema over purely plot-driven cinema**, but I still enjoy strong genre films when the genre is being used to explore character or ideas.
* I’m especially interested in films that have something to say about **mortality, identity, loneliness, memory, fate, self-invention, or how people construct meaning from their lives**.
* I appreciate films that **trust the audience** rather than explaining every emotion or interpretation.
* I like discovering **less popular or under-discussed films**, not just obvious canonical recommendations.
* I particularly value the portrayal of **women and female interiority**, especially when women are written as psychologically complex people rather than simply serving the protagonist.
* My taste is not limited to one genre. I can like comedy, romance, mystery, crime, surrealism, thriller, drama, or adventure when they contain the emotional, formal, or thematic qualities above.

Some films that are especially useful references for my taste:
*My Night at Maud's, Groundhog Day, The Apartment, Portrait of a Lady on Fire, Blue Velvet, Mulholland Drive, Amélie, Magnolia, A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night, Maelström, Y Tu Mamá También, Petite Maman, Frances Ha, When Harry Met Sally, Sleepless in Seattle, Bend It Like Beckham, Raising Arizona, Adventures in Babysitting.*

Important: treat these as **patterns in my taste, not rigid rules**. I can love a film even when it doesn't fit them, and I don't want every recommendation forced into the same psychological interpretation.
